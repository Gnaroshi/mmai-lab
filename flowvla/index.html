Learning 3D scene dynamics during training.
Deploying the original VLA with no additional inference cost.

Overview

FlowVLA teaches existing vision-language-action policies to connect 3D scene motion with robot control through training-only flow supervision.

Abstract

Vision-language-action models inherit strong semantic priors from 2D image-text pretraining, but these representations do not explicitly capture how a scene moves during manipulation. FlowVLA uses temporally consistent 3D motion reconstructed from RGB demonstrations as a training-only supervision signal. A point-level objective teaches the action representation to predict the future displacement of motion-salient scene points. A TCP-level objective learns to aggregate these displacements into the robot’s Tool-Center-Point motion, connecting scene dynamics to control. The auxiliary heads and 3D geometric inputs are removed at inference. Across Seer and π₀.₅, experiments on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and a UR5e robot show improvements in manipulation success, robustness, and data efficiency while preserving the original inference pathway. The method provides a modular way to learn manipulation-relevant 3D dynamics without introducing an additional input modality at deployment.

Method

3D dynamics as training-only supervision

FlowVLA attaches two auxiliary objectives to an existing VLA’s action representation. The reconstruction pipeline supplies privileged 3D motion targets during training; the policy retains its original visual, language, and proprioceptive inputs at deployment.

01 · Construct the supervision

Reconstruction-Guided 3D Flow Construction

VDPM reconstructs temporally consistent 3D trajectories from RGB demonstration sequences. Points remain anchored to the first frame’s pixel grid, so each trajectory follows the same scene point over time. We transform these trajectories from the reference-camera frame into the robot-base frame and compute adjacent-frame displacements to form 3D flow targets.

Magnitude-based Top-K selection retains the points with the largest average displacement over the training window. This concentrates supervision on moving regions without requiring semantic masks, object annotations, or contact labels. The reported Seer configuration uses K = 2,048 points.

Reference-anchored trajectories keep point identities consistent over time. Magnitude-based Top-K selection focuses supervision on moving regions.

02 · Learn scene motion and control together

Dual-Level 3D Flow Learning

Point-Level Flow

For each future prediction step, an auxiliary branch combines the action latent with the selected points’ 3D coordinates and predicts a displacement for every point. A Smooth L1 loss compares these predictions with reconstruction-derived flow targets, encouraging the action representation to encode future scene motion.

TCP-Level Flow

A learned weighting head assigns a softmax-normalized weight to each selected point. The weighted sum of predicted point displacements estimates the robot’s TCP displacement and is supervised with the ground-truth TCP motion from the demonstration. This objective connects scene-level motion to the control-relevant movement of the end-effector.

Δx̂tcp = ∑u r(u) · ΔP̂(u)

03 · Keep the original policy at inference

Joint training, unchanged inference

The action loss trains the original policy, while point-level and TCP-level flow losses shape its action representation. After training, the auxiliary heads and all 3D geometric inputs are discarded.

Ltotal = Laction + λpointLpoint + λtcpLtcp

Real-world experiments

Simulation experiments

Improvements across VLA backbones

We evaluate two different policy architectures: Seer, which uses an MLP action policy, and π₀.₅, which uses a flow-matching action policy. FlowVLA is attached only during training. LIBERO measures manipulation performance; LIBERO-Plus tests the same trained policies under unseen camera, robot, language, lighting, background, noise, and layout perturbations.

Analysis

Why does 3D flow help?

Both supervision levels contribute. Removing either the point-level loss or the TCP-level loss reduces success, while coordinate alignment, temporal correspondence, and motion-based point selection each improve the quality of supervision. Dynamic 3D flow also outperforms equally sized auxiliary heads trained with 2D optical flow or static 3D points.

Data Efficiency and Computational Cost

FlowVLA improves success across the evaluated training-data fractions. In the reported Seer analysis, the auxiliary heads add 0.34% parameters and policy training takes approximately 1.26× as long, while inference computation is unchanged. The offline reconstruction step is performed once and its targets can be cached for subsequent experiments.

Offline reconstruction is a separate preprocessing cost: approximately 4 hours for LIBERO-Long in the reported setup. The reconstructed targets can be cached.

Limitations

FlowVLA needs offline 3D reconstruction, and supervision quality depends on the reconstructed trajectories. Motion magnitude provides a simple way to select active regions, but it does not guarantee that every selected point is relevant to the task. Here, world dynamics refers to manipulation-relevant 3D scene motion; the experiments do not establish general understanding of forces or contact mechanics.
